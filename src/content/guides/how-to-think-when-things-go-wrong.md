title: How to think when things go wrong
---

Things go wrong. Plans fail. People change their minds. Money gets lost. Jobs disappear. Health changes. Something you were counting on suddenly is no longer there.

You do not have to pretend that is fine.

In my Position [**“The plan failed. So what?”**](/positions/the-plan-failed-so-what/), I argued that planning matters, but the real test begins when Plan A dies.

**A plan is only a route. It is not the point.**

This Guide is the practical follow-up:

> **When the plan fails, how do you think clearly enough to decide what happens next?**

You can be angry, disappointed or afraid and still think clearly.

> **Agency is not control over what happened. It is deciding what to do with what remains.**

## Step 1: Separate fact from reaction

Start with what actually happened.

**Fact:** I didn't get the job.  
**Reaction:** Nothing ever works out for me.

Both may feel real, but they are not the same thing.

Do not force yourself to feel better. Just make sure your emotions are not quietly turning into facts.

## Step 2: Accept what is now true

Once something has happened, stop making decisions based on how things were supposed to go.

Ask what is still available to you: money, time, skills, relationships, options and obligations.

> **You do not have to like the new reality. But you do have to make decisions based on it.**

## Step 3: Recheck the goal

Ask whether the **route** failed or whether the **destination** itself no longer makes sense.

Sometimes Plan B is simply another route.

Sometimes the failed plan tells you that the destination should change too.

There is no virtue in continuing simply because you already spent time, money or effort.

**Sometimes the correct next move is to wait. Sometimes it is to quit.**

## Step 4: Choose the next sensible move

Do not rush to replace one failed plan with another elaborate plan.

Ask:

> **What is the next sensible thing I can do from here?**

Act if the answer is clear. Wait if you need more information. Change direction if the route no longer works. Quit if continuing no longer makes sense.

## Step 5: Move, then reassess

Once you choose a direction, move.

Then look again.

Did the situation improve? Did you learn something new? Does the direction still make sense?

> **Pick a direction. Move. Look up. Correct. Continue.**

The new plan is allowed to change too.

## What I learned

When things go wrong, the job is not to stay positive.

**It is to stay clear enough to decide what to do next.**

> **You don't have to like what happened. You just have to stop letting the old plan make the next decision.**

Move from:

> **“This wasn't supposed to happen.”**

to:

> **“Okay. What now?”**
