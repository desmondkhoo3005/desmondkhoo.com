> **My position:** Life contains wayyyyy more chance than we like to admit. Build enough structure to protect what matters, tilt the odds where you can, then take some bets. Don't spend your whole life trying to eliminate uncertainty. That's part of the fun.

Life is a game of chance.

Actually, you started as one.

Millions of possibilities had to line up just right before one sperm met one egg and somehow—

**Hello. You.**

And it continues.

Where you are born. Who your parents are. Who you meet.

Which opportunity appears. Which one disappears.

Which illness hits. Which investment works. Which relationship unexpectedly changes your life.

We like to think we are fully in control because control feels comforting.

We aren't.

But that doesn't mean we are helpless either.

## Tilt the table

In a game of dice, we cannot control the result.

But the least we can do is tilt the table a little to make the odds more favourable.

Plan. Save. Train. Learn.

Take care of your health. Build skills. Meet more people. Do your homework.

Create optionality.

**You are not guaranteeing the result. You are changing the odds.**

And sometimes, despite all that:

you still lose.

That's part of playing.

The mistake is thinking uncertainty means we should wait until everything is clear.

Sometimes you just need enough information to say:

**Okay. Good enough. Let's see what happens.**

Take the job. Start the business. Make the investment. Talk to the person.

Try something you have absolutely no idea whether you will be good at.

Not recklessly.

Just accept that sometimes the only way to find out what happens next is to actually move.

## Every game has rules

Of course, don't read this as:

> Desmond said take more risks.

Please.

Every game has rules.

Mine are quite simple.

First:

> **Protect the people who depend on you.**

Once somebody relies on you, your downside is no longer entirely yours.

Don't bet the family's survival money. Don't blow up everyone's security because you wanted some excitement.

Build the floor first.

Second:

> **Don't make strangers pay for your stupidity.**

Take whatever personal bets you want.

But your thrill should not come at somebody else's expense.

Don't put innocent people in danger. Don't dump avoidable messes onto colleagues. Don't keep the upside for yourself while somebody else carries all the downside.

**The point of structure is not to remove adventure. It is to buy yourself enough safety to have some.**

If you can satisfy those two rules?

Then honestly:

**Play a little.**

## Isn't that the beauty of life?

Someone could ask:

> Life already gives us enough uncertainty. Why deliberately add more?

Fair question.

But I don't want to know every chapter beforehand.

I don't want every decision to have a guaranteed outcome.

I don't want to optimise my entire existence until nothing unexpected is allowed to happen.

Structure gives life stability.

But the point of stability cannot be to spend the rest of your life hiding inside it.

Sometimes take a chance.

*Sometimes you kena.*

Sometimes you win.

Sometimes something completely different happens and you wonder how the hell you ended up there.

Good.

At least you were living.

So build the structure.

Tilt the table.

Protect the downside.

Then stop trying to control every last variable.

**Life is a game of chance.**

**Have some fun, PLAY it.**
